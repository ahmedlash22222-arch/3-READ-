Spaced Repetition:
Why You Forget
& How To Stop

You don't have a bad memory. You have a normal brain. You are just reviewing at the wrong time.

You study for 3 hours. Next day you remember almost nothing.
Sound familiar? It's not your fault. It's science.

Your brain is designed to forget. If you don't remind it, it deletes. Spaced Repetition is how you tell your brain: "Keep this, this is important."

You don't forget because you are stupid.
You forget because you didn't review at the right time.

What Is Spaced Repetition?

Instead of studying a topic 5 times in one day, you study it 5 times spread out.

Day 1: Learn it.
Day 2: Recall it.
Day 4: Recall it again.
Day 7: Recall it again.
Day 14: Recall it again.

Each time, your memory gets stronger. After Day 7, it stays for months.

The Schedule That Works

WhenWhat To Do
Day 1Learn new topic. Read + make 10 flashcards.
Day 2Close book. Recall from memory. 10 min only.
Day 4Recall again. If you forget, check book quickly, then recall again.
Day 7Recall again. Now it's in long-term memory.
Day 14Final check. If you know it now, you will know it in exam.

Why Cramming Fails

Cramming feels good because you recognize the answer. But recognition is not recall.

  • Cramming: You read 5 times in one night. You feel "I know it". Exam next week = gone.
  • Spaced: You recall 5 times in 2 weeks. It feels harder. Exam next month = still there.

Hard feels bad, but hard is what makes memory stick.

How To Use It With Anki

Anki does the schedule for you automatically. You don't need to remember Day 2, 4, 7. Anki will show you the card at the right time.

Your only job: Do your Anki every day. Even 20 minutes is enough. Don't skip 2 days in a row.

Your Action Today - 10 Minutes

1. Pick ONE topic you learned yesterday.
2. Close your book.
3. Write everything you remember on paper.
4. Open book, check what you missed.
5. That's it. You just did Spaced Repetition.

Common Mistake

Don't re-read to review. Re-reading feels easy but does nothing. Always recall - close book and try to remember. That's the test that builds memory.
