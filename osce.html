How To Study For OSCE
Without Panic

OSCE is not about knowing everything. It's about showing what you know, in order, in 5 minutes.

You know the disease. But in OSCE, you freeze. You forget what to say. You forget to wash hands. You run out of time.

OSCE is a skill. You can practice it like football.

OSCE is not a knowledge exam.
It's a performance exam.

The 5-Minute Station Structure

0:00 - 0:30: Wash hands, introduce yourself, "Hello, I'm Dr. [Name], may I examine you?"
0:30 - 1:00: Take history: "What brought you here?"
1:00 - 3:30: Examine / Do the task
3:30 - 4:30: Explain findings + plan
4:30 - 5:00: Thank patient, wash hands, close.

If you follow this structure, you will not get lost, even if you forget a detail.

The 3 Secrets Examiners Love

  • 1. Communication > Knowledge: A student who explains clearly with 70% knowledge passes. A student who knows 100% but cannot explain fails.
  • 2. Always say what you are doing: "Now I am checking your pulse." "Now I am listening to your chest." Examiner cannot read your mind. Speak your actions.
  • 3. Safety first: Wash hands, ask about allergies, ask "Are you in pain?" Examiners give marks for safety.

How To Practice - No Partner Needed

1. Mirror Practice: Stand in front of mirror, explain a disease in 2 minutes. Watch your face. Are you confident?

2. Phone Timer: Set 5 min timer. Do a full station alone, speaking out loud. If you finish in 5 min, you are ready.

3. Teach A Friend: Teach your friend a station. If you can teach it, you can do it in OSCE.

4. Use NotebookLM: Ask: "Make an OSCE script for Appendicitis, 5 minutes." Practice that script 3 times.

Common OSCE Stations - Sudan

History: Chest pain, Abdominal pain, Fever, Cough
Examination: Cardiovascular, Respiratory, Abdominal, Cranial nerves
Procedure: Cannulation, Suturing, Blood pressure, ECG
Counselling: Diabetes, Hypertension, Asthma inhaler, Breaking bad news

Make 1 script for each. Practice 1 per day. In 2 weeks you covered all.

What To Do If You Forget In OSCE?

Don't freeze. Say: "Let me think about that for a moment." Take a breath. Go back to structure. Ask patient a question to buy time: "Can you tell me more about your pain?"

Examiner wants to see you handle pressure, not be perfect.
