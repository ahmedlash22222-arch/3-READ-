How To Remember Pharmacology
Without Crying

Don't memorize 500 drugs. Learn 30 families.

Pharmacology feels impossible because you try to memorize drug by drug. Stop.

Learn by family. If you know the family, you know 90% of the drugs in it.

Don't learn drugs.
Learn families.

The Suffix Trick

-pril: ACE Inhibitors - Enalapril, Lisinopril - Lowers BP, cough, angioedema
-sartan: ARBs - Losartan, Valsartan - Lowers BP, no cough
-olol: Beta Blockers - Atenolol, Propranolol - Slows heart, asthma warning
-dipine: Calcium Channel Blockers - Amlodipine - Lowers BP, ankle swelling
-statin: Statins - Atorvastatin - Lowers cholesterol, muscle pain
-floxacin: Fluoroquinolones - Ciprofloxacin - Antibiotics, tendon rupture
-prazole: PPIs - Omeprazole - Reduces acid

See the ending? You already know the drug.

The 4 Questions For Every Drug

For every family, ask only 4 things:

  • 1. What does it do? Mechanism in 1 line
  • 2. When do we use it? 2-3 uses
  • 3. What is dangerous? 2 side effects + 1 contraindication
  • 4. What is unique? 1 thing that makes it different from others in family

If you can answer these 4, you know pharmacology.

Make A 1-Line Story

Example: "Mr. PRIL coughs because his ACE is blocked."
Example: "Amlodipine makes ankles swell like balloons."

Silly stories stick. Boring lists don't.
