ARTICLE 5.7 • 6 MIN READ • EXAM STRATEGY

Interleaving:
Stop Studying One Topic
At A Time

Mixed practice gives +43% exam scores. This is why you fail even though you studied.

You study Diabetes for 3 hours. You feel you know it. In exam, question mixes Diabetes + Hypertension + Kidney disease. You panic.

Why? Because you studied in blocks. Exam is mixed. You must practice mixed.

Exam is not blocked.
So your study should not be blocked.

Blocked vs Interleaved

Blocked (Bad):
Monday: Only Diabetes
Tuesday: Only Hypertension
Wednesday: Only Asthma

Interleaved (Good):
Monday: Diabetes + Hypertension + Asthma - mixed
Tuesday: Same 3 topics, mixed questions
Wednesday: Same 3, mixed again

Interleaved feels harder and slower. But in exam, you are 43% better. Because exam is also mixed.

Why It Works

When you study one topic, your brain knows the answer type. No thinking needed.

When you mix, your brain must first ask: "What type of problem is this? What do I use here?" That's exactly what exam asks.

It's like football training. You don't practice only passing for 3 hours. You practice passing + shooting + defending mixed. Match is mixed.

How To Do It - Simple Plan

Your 1-Hour Study:

20 min: Topic A - Read + Recall
20 min: Topic B - Read + Recall
20 min: Mixed Questions - A + B mixed, no book

Example: 20 min Heart Failure, 20 min Pneumonia, 20 min mixed quiz on both.

How To Mix Questions

  • Use Anki with 2 decks mixed together.
  • Make 5 questions from each topic, shuffle them on paper.
  • Use past papers - they are already mixed!

Your Action Today

1. Pick 2 topics you already studied.
2. Write 5 questions from each.
3. Shuffle them.
4. Answer them mixed, book closed.
5. That's interleaving.

Important: Interleaving Is For Review, Not First Learn

First time you learn a topic, learn it alone. Then after you know basics, mix it. Don't mix 3 new topics you never learned.
