Flashcards That
Actually Work

Most flashcards are useless. Here is how to make cards that make you remember.

You made 100 flashcards. You review them. You still fail.

Because your cards are wrong. Let's fix them in 10 minutes.

1 fact per card.
Question, not heading.
Short answer, not paragraph.

Rule 1: 1 Fact Per Card

BAD: Front: "Heart Failure" Back: Long paragraph about everything.

Why bad? Your brain doesn't know what to recall. Too much.

GOOD: Front: "What is the most common cause of Heart Failure?" Back: "Ischemic heart disease"

One question, one answer. Fast. Clear.

Rule 2: Ask, Don't Copy

BAD: Front: "Diabetes symptoms" Back: "Polyuria, polydipsia..."

GOOD: Front: "Patient is thirsty, urinates a lot, losing weight. What is diagnosis?" Back: "Diabetes"

Second one is like exam. First one is just copying book.

Rule 3: Comparison Cards - Best For Medicine

Medicine is full of similar diseases. Make cards that compare them:

Card: "Asthma vs COPD - Which one is reversible?"
Answer: "Asthma is reversible, COPD is not."

Card: "Type 1 vs Type 2 Diabetes - Which one needs insulin from Day 1?"
Answer: "Type 1"

Exams love comparisons. Make 5 comparison cards per topic.

Rule 4: Use Image Occlusion For Anatomy

Take a diagram of heart. Cover one label. Try to recall it. That's Image Occlusion. Anki has this free. Best for Anatomy.

Rule 5: Use Your Own Words

Don't copy Davidson word-for-word. Write in your simple words. If you write it, you remember it.

Paper Cards Or Anki?

  • Anki: Best. It does spacing for you. Use it for long-term.
  • Paper cards: Good if no phone / no electricity. Put them in your pocket, review in bus.
  • Both: Make cards on paper first, then put best ones in Anki.

Your Action Today - 15 Minutes

1. Pick 1 topic: Pneumonia.
2. Make 10 cards, 1 fact each.
3. Make 2 comparison cards.
4. Review them tomorrow.
5. Done.
