Feynman Technique:
Explain Like To A Patient

If you can't explain it simply, you don't know it. This is how doctors really learn.

Richard Feynman was a Nobel Prize physicist. His secret was simple: If you can't explain it to a 12-year-old, you don't understand it.

Medical students have the same problem. We memorize words like "nephrotic syndrome" but we can't explain it to a patient. In OSCE, that fails.

Don't memorize words.
Understand the story, then tell the story.

The 4 Steps

Step 1: Pick One Topic

Not a whole chapter. One disease. Example: Diabetes.

Step 2: Explain It Like To A Patient

Talk out loud, in simple words, no medical jargon. Pretend your patient is in front of you:

"Diabetes means your body cannot use sugar properly. Sugar stays in your blood and makes you thirsty, you urinate a lot, you feel tired. Over time it damages your eyes, kidneys, and feet."

If you can say that without looking at book, you understand it.

Step 3: Find Your Gaps

When you stop, when you say "uhh...", when you use a big word you don't understand - that's your gap. Go back to book and learn ONLY that gap.

Step 4: Simplify Again

Explain again, even simpler. Use an analogy.

  • Heart failure: Heart is a pump. Pump is weak. Water builds up in lungs and legs.
  • Asthma: Airways are tubes. Tubes get narrow. Air cannot go out. Patient wheezes.

Why This Works For Exams?

1. OSCE: You must explain to patients. This is practice.
2. Written exam: You remember stories, not lists.
3. Ward: You actually become a doctor, not a memorizer.

Your Action Today - 10 Minutes

1. Pick ONE disease: Hypertension.
2. Explain it in 1 minute out loud, like to your mother.
3. Where did you stop? Go learn that part.
4. Explain again. Done.

Don't Do This Mistake

Don't explain to yourself in your head. You must SPEAK out loud. Your mouth shows gaps your brain hides.
